What happens when customers click help before booking?

Every person who opened the Wiom app for the first time, followed into the booking flow — what they asked, whether we answered, and where we still lose them.

Periods  8–30 Jun vs 1–22 Jul 2026 New users  48,118  /  49,800 Messages  17,327 People  6,673

July was better for new users on almost every measure — fewer needed help, and far more of those who did got an answer.

Out of every 1,000 first-time app opens, 74.0 sent a message in chat in June against 62.5 in July. Dead ends fell further still — from 12.1 to 6.2 per 1,000. Of the new users who did open chat, 90.1% got an answer, up from 83.6%. Every major topic fell, and five of the six pages improved.

One thing got worse, and it is specific. Frustration is the only measure that moved against us on both counts — 5.5% to 7.1% of the new users who chatted, and 4.1 to 4.5 per 1,000 new users. Almost all of it sits on the QR / payment screen, where it nearly tripled. That is one fixable page, not a general decline.

ScopeExisting customers removed from every figure.

This report covers new users only

Customers who were already on Wiom — 159 in June and 984 in July — have been removed from every number here. They arrive on booking pages with outages, forgotten passwords and refund questions, and none of these six screens is built to help them. They are a separate audience with separate problems, and leaving them in distorted the picture for the people we are trying to win.

We identify them from what they type — mentions of an existing connection, a password, or internet that is already down — plus the app's own "already a customer" route. The same test is applied to both windows.

Half of chip-tappers still type a question afterwards

Chips carry about half of all pre-booking messages (48% June, 51% July) and reach the majority of chatters (66% → 73% tap at least one). The most-tapped buttons are the fundamentals — how many days to install, what is the recharge-wala plan, what is Wiom — exactly the questions a first-time user has. But the canned answer often isn’t the end: of 5,443 new users who tapped a chip, 2,646 (49%) went on to type a free-text follow-up. The chip opens the conversation; it rarely closes it. The July win came partly from a new ‘I already use Wiom’ chip (existingUserDoor, 0 → 856 taps) that routes existing customers out of the new-user flow — a good example of a chip removing the wrong-audience noise rather than adding to it.

Two-thirds tap a chip — and pre-booking chips resolve better than post-booking ones

About 66–73% of new users tap at least one chip, and of those, 43–40% then type a free-text follow-up. That follow-up rate is lower than the post-booking install chat (where 60%+ of tappers type again) — the pre-booking chips answer a fixed informational question (what is Wiom, how many days, what plans) more completely than the post-booking status chip, which can only describe a stage. Still, a meaningful share type anyway, so the canned answers are a strong start, not the last word.

Booking-home is where the questions get asked

The booking home / landing page dominates chip taps — it is where almost everyone lands and where the fundamental questions (what is this, how many days, what does it cost) get tapped before anyone commits. These answers carry the first impression

Because roughly half of all pre-booking messages are chip taps, this short list of canned replies is doing a large share of the company’s early customer communication. Where we need to improve the communication

Four gaps. Quotes are real customer messages, spelling exactly as written.

01

Explain how recharging works

1,883 new users asked · worst repeat rate of any topic

It improved — 20.7 to 17.8 people per 1,000 new users — but it is still the question people ask over and over. Most type it out rather than tapping a suggested question, which tells us the page has not already answered it.

  • mujhe mera recharge karna hai Wi-Fi ka
  • recharge kaise kare

Change: Put a worked example of the recharge cycle on the landing and How-to-get-started pages: what you pay, when, and what happens if you don't.
02

Fix cash payment on the QR screen

Frustration tripled: 6.1% → 17.8%

The sharpest deterioration anywhere in this data, and it is happening to genuine prospective customers. Half of everyone on the QR page asks about paying cash while looking at a QR code. It is also our smallest page, so it is the cheapest thing on this list to fix.

  • me abhi payment nhi kar sakta hu
  • why your scan don't show me
  • cash on delivery
  • maine 45 ka richarge kr diya hai pr abhi bhe richarge pendin show ho raha h
Change: State the accepted payment methods before the QR screen, and either offer a cash path or say plainly why there isn't one.
03

Explain the price change from ₹500 to ₹565

Checkout: the only page that clearly got worse

Messages each rose 2.31 to 2.73 and frustration rose 5.2% to 7.6%. Customers who remember the old price read the new one as a trick. They are not asking what it costs — they are asking why it changed.

  • लेकिन रिचार्ज तो पहले 500 का था अब 565 कर दिया ऐसा क्यु

  • pahle to 500 ko utha tha ab jyada charge badha rahe ho kya company walon Tum
Change: Say on the page what changed and what the customer gets for it. Silence is being read as a con.
04

Say who brings the box, and what it costs

Recurring on How-to-get-started and Checkout

New users cannot tell whether the router is free, refundable, bought, or brought by an engineer. It surfaces as unanswered questions rather than a named topic, which is why it is easy to miss in the totals.

Change: One line on the cost pages: who brings the equipment, what it costs, and whether the money comes back.

LimitsWhat this data cannot answer.

What this doesn't tell us

  • How many new users reached each page. The gap in the funnel. Without it we cannot say what share of people on a page needed to chat — only what happened once they did. The most valuable missing number.
  • Whether the answers were any good. We see only whether people came back, gave up, or got angry. The chat's reply is recorded for just under half of messages.
  • Whether chatting affects buying. Pre-booking IDs do not match the post-booking system, so we cannot follow a new user from a chat through to a completed booking.
  • These are two different groups of people. Almost nobody appears in both windows.
Glossary

Words used here

New user
Someone opening the Wiom app for the first time. 48,118 in June, 49,800 across 1–22 July. Customers already on Wiom are excluded from this whole report.
Dead end
Someone typed a question and the chat had no answer to give.
Messages each
Messages divided by people, across the whole window — not per session. The median is 2 and 36% send only one, so the mean of ~2.6 is pulled up by a small tail.
Asked something substantive
Sent at least one message that was not purely a greeting or an “ok / thanks”. 97.5% of people who message do this.
Per 1,000 new users
Divided by first-time app opens, so a busier month doesn't automatically look worse.
Repeat
The same person asking the same thing three or more times — a strong sign the first answer didn't work.
Upset
The share of messages the system flagged as frustrated or angry.
